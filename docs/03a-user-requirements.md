# CrossSafe user requirements

What users need, written from their side. Product requirements that meet these needs are in CRS-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix CRS-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). They are drawn from the users and stakeholders in CRS-PRB-001 and are proposals until they are checked with a partner (co-design, TRL 4). Method: STANDARDS section 22.

*Table 1. User requirements.*

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Drivers notice the warning and yield | Drivers see the flashing indications early and in all light, so that they slow and yield to a waiting pedestrian | High | Pedestrian; Driver (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Warning when someone waits, on both sides | The warning starts within half a second of a trigger on either side and only when someone is waiting or presses the button | High | Pedestrian; School or market community |
| UR-003 | Usability | Anyone can operate it and see it is on | Children, older people and people with disabilities can reach and press the button, and know from sound, touch and a light that the warning is on | High | Pedestrian, including children, older people and people with disabilities |
| UR-004 | Performance | The warning lasts long enough, and no longer | The warning runs long enough to cross at a slow walking pace and ends by itself, so that drivers do not learn to ignore it | High | Driver; Pedestrian |
| UR-005 | Environment and durability | Works without mains and through poor-sun weeks | The crossing keeps working off the grid, in the worst sun month and through several dull days | High | School or market community |
| UR-006 | Regulatory | Meets local rules and survives the weather | The device can be accepted by the road authority: right indication size and pattern, and it survives wind, heat, rain and dust for years | High | Road authority or municipal engineer |
| UR-007 | Safety | A fault never leaves a misleading warning | Faults are shown and logged, and the device never flashes continuously or goes dark without notice | High | Road authority or municipal engineer |
| UR-008 | Deployment and assembly | Fits an existing pole and installs quickly | A two-person crew fits it to a common pole in about two hours, without a bucket truck where possible | Medium | Road authority or municipal engineer; Local technician |
| UR-009 | Cost | Costs little to buy | The parts for one assembly cost no more than the value-engineering target, so a school or market group can fund a crossing | High | School or market community; Road authority |
| UR-010 | Maintenance | Built and repaired with ordinary tools and parts | A local workshop can make the made parts, and a technician can service and replace parts with hand tools and a multimeter | Medium | Local technician (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-011 | Regulatory | No images or audio of people are kept | The device records and sends only counts, faults and battery state | Medium | Pedestrian; Road authority |
| UR-012 | Environment and durability | Resists theft and vandalism | Batteries and electronics are out of casual reach and the fixings need tools | Medium | School or market community; Road authority |
| UR-013 | Safety | The lights are not harmful to look at | People standing beside the device or driving past are not dazzled or put at risk by the flashing | Medium | Pedestrian; Driver |
| UR-014 | Performance | The warning is seen early enough to stop | At road speeds of 30 to 60 km/h a driver sees the warning at a distance longer than the stopping distance | High | Driver |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
